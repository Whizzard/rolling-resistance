01Side view

The side frame is a triangle of mast and diagonal: the drum hangs from the bearing block on the diagonal, the swing-arm pivot sits on the mast's slider. Because the diagonal is inclined, the offset a = 200 mm between drum axle and pivot comes about by itself. The pivot lies 30 mm above the wheel axle (dropout 10 mm below the fork rail) — close enough to axle height that the tangential force hardly changes the wheel load, see 05b.

02Front view

Looking in +x; mast and diagonal lie behind (dashed), the shaft runs freely past them. With a 100 mm drum width everything moves closer together: bearing spacing 180 instead of 340, cross member 900 instead of 1000 — and the shaft no longer needs a shoulder: Ø30 across the drum width, where the bending moment acts, Ø15 journals in the bearings, where it is zero. The drum is clamped at both end discs. The motor hangs on the shaft end via a flange and a radially compliant coupling; the cradle bearings then carry only the motor (≈ 60 N instead of ≈ 650 N) and their hysteresis drops from 9.8 to 1.2 mNm.

03Detail torque balance

Lever arm 70.0 mm as set in 03-parameters: the worst case with ramp (4.34 Nm) lands at 62 N, i.e. 63 % of the full scale of the 10 kg cell. The calibration hole at 140.0 mm takes a known weight — 1 kg there is 1.373 Nm.

04Rotating shaft: the problem and the fix

A rotating shaft alternates between tension and compression in every fibre once per revolution — at 333 rpm that is 20 000 load cycles per hour. So it is not the yield strength that governs but the fatigue strength, and that depends on the notch effect. That is the whole difference from a stationary axle: a factor of 2 to 3 in the permissible stress.

Five details the fatigue strength depends on

  • No keyway. βk = 2.5 halves the permissible stress. Clamping hubs or locking assemblies on the Ø30 — friction-locked, backlash-free, self-centring.
  • Undercut at the shoulder, R ≥ 2 mm, not turned sharp-edged. The shoulder lies at y = ±150, i.e. 20 mm beside the bearing, where only 6.1 Nm act.
  • Journals turned and polished (Rz ≤ 6.3). The surface factor 0.85 is in the calculation; a turning groove across the axis eats it up.
  • No loosely slid-on sleeve as reinforcement instead of the shoulder — under alternating bending it causes fretting at its edges, the classic crack initiation. If a sleeve, then with a locking assembly and ending on the low-moment section.
  • Drum centred and balanced. 100 g of residual imbalance at r = 0.45 m is, at 34.9 rad/s, about 55 N of rotating additional load — 9 % of the static load, and it rotates with the shaft.

04bWhat else deviates from the proposal

  • Bearings stay 6902 — 15×28×7, 307 N per bearing, L10 ≈ 110 000 h. Alternative 6002-2RS (15×32×9, L10 ≈ 300 000 h): same bore, hence same friction torque, but a wider seat. The load-dependent friction torque grows with the bore, not with the bearing size: 4.7 mNm at Ø15 versus 7.8 mNm at Ø25, i.e. 0.63 instead of 1.06 % of the road-tyre signal.
  • Two posts with a diagonal instead of one profile tube. A single post would have to be drilled through by the shaft, exactly at the load introduction. And without a diagonal a 40×40 bends by 5.6 mm under 100 N of disturbing force at the top — with the diagonal by 0.06 mm.
  • Sliding weight 10 kg, not 5. For 417 N at a = 200 mm, 10 kg needs a lever of 800 mm; with 5 kg it would be 1700 mm. 5 kg only covers up to 221 N.
  • Feet 700 mm instead of 400–500. The drum reaches 477 mm forward; a foot ending at 300 stands behind its front edge. With 400 mm forward the frame only tips at 281 N of pressure on the end of the swing arm.

05bPivot at wheel-axle height: the one drawback

Pivot and wheel axle at the same height means: the link is horizontal, the wheel axle moves vertically, contact point and lever a stay put across all tyres and pressures. The price: the tangential force T acts rwheel below the pivot and changes the wheel load by ΔN = T · rwheel/a.

The error scales with Crr itself and therefore distorts the ranking — at up to 2.8 % it is too large uncorrected. Three ways, each sufficient: (1) by calculation, Neff = N₀ ∓ Froll · rwheel/a — all three quantities are measured, the correction itself is accurate to 0.01 %; (2) measure the wheel load directly (bearing brackets on load cells), then it disappears entirely; (3) run in both directions of rotation and average, the term changes sign and drops out. The direction of rotation then becomes a logged value.

The alternative — pivot at contact-patch height, T without a lever — has the opposite drawback: the wheel axle then moves on an inclined arc, and every tyre deflection shifts the contact point. 15 mm of deflection (MTB at 2 bar) moves it 25 mm and falsifies a by 7.4 % — that would have to be readjusted to x = 0 for every tyre and cannot be corrected from the measurement.

06dSlot nuts for bearing seats and dropouts

The slot nuts from the bracket sets are used up by the brackets. Everything screwed into a slot as a plate or holder needs its own — and specifically the type with spring plate, swivelling, with slot guide: only that one can be inserted afterwards into a fully bolted frame. A slide-in nut has to be pushed in from the end of the profile and is then useless.

Design rule for the bearing seats: a 40×40 profile has one slot per face, centred. A plate on one face therefore has all its screws in one line — and a line of screws takes no moment about itself, the plate tips out of the face. Make bearing seats therefore as an angle plate across two profile faces (slot on top + slot on the side) or secure them with an additional bracket: two screw lines at right angles, then the seat is tilt-stiff.

The most heavily loaded slot-nut joint is the dropout: for OLD 100 it cantilevers up to 95 mm inward and carries 208 N → M = 19.8 Nm. A screw pair 60 mm apart gives 330 N pull-out force per slot nut, 100 mm apart only 198 N. So points far apart and four instead of two. The permissible pull-out force is not on the product page — ask the seller.

Do not confuse I-type and B-type. Both are called "slot 8" and have an 8 mm slot opening, but the slot chamber is shaped differently. The shop has separate subcategories "Nutensteine Nut 8 I-Typ" and "Nutensteine Nut 8 B-Typ"; the chosen profile is I-type (item-compatible). The product page of the slot nut with spring plate talks about B-type suitability — clarify before ordering, otherwise the pack will not fit the slot.
